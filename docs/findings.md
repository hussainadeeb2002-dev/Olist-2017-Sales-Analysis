The findings show that Olist's product-level revenue was driven by a combination of high-volume categories and categories whose revenue contribution was disproportionately high relative to their item-volume contribution.

This means that product category performance cannot be explained by sales volume alone. While categories such as **bed_bath_table** generated substantial revenue alongside high sales volume, categories such as **watches_gifts** and **computers** generated a larger share of revenue relative to their share of items sold.

The next step should investigate how these product-category contributions changed, particularly during **November**, when Olist experienced its largest monthly revenue increase.
